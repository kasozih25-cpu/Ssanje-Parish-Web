Our Story

About Ssanje Parish

History

The parish was formally established in 1987 during a period of steady pastoral expansion within the diocese under Bishop Adrian Kivumbi Ddungu. Prior to becoming an independent parish, the community was served as a sub-station, receiving sacraments from older neighboring parishes in the Kyotera and Rakai zones.

Over time, the parish grew significantly in structural importance, eventually becoming the headquarters of the Ssanje Deanery. Today, this deanery manages an expansive territory comprising 6 parishes and 77 sub-parishes.

Parish Patron Saint

The parish is dedicated to St. John the Baptist. As the patron saint, his life serves as the theological foundation for the parish's focus on evangelization, baptismal renewal, and preparing the community to live according to the Gospel.

Mission & Vision

🎯 Mission

Holistic evangelization, basic Christian community growth, healthcare excellence, and environmental stewardship.

👁️ Vision

To be an evangelized and evangelizing Church, United, Co-ordinate, and Self-Sustaining, Rooted in the Basic Christian Community.

👨‍💼 Parish Leadership

The administrative structure functions under the overarching authority of the Diocese of Masaka, led by Bishop Serverus Jjumba.

Agricultural Training

Ssanje Farm School

Practical agricultural skills for integral human development, rooted in self-reliance.

About the Farm School

Ssanje Agricultural Farm Institute is located along the Masaka-Mutukula highway in Ssanje trading center, 17 km from Kyotera town. The institute was started by Masaka Diocese and later mandated by the Ministry of Education and Sports in 2019.

The institute is rooted in the spirit of self-reliance — the ability to rely on and utilize our own resources, capabilities, talents, and efforts to grow and develop. The goal is achieved through offering persons practical occupational skills, competence-based assessment, and certification.

🎯 Mission

To offer agribusiness training and skilling for employability, agro-entrepreneurship, and integral development.

👁️ Vision

To be a leading institution in the provision of innovative practical agricultural skills inspired by values for integral human development.

Healthcare Services

St. Jude Ssanje Health Centre III

Providing quality healthcare to the Ssanje community and surrounding areas since 2003.

Facility Overview

Name: ST. JUDES SANJE HEALTH CENTRE III
Level: HEALTH CENTRE III
Type: PRIVATE NOT-FOR-PROFIT (PNFP)
Ownership: MASAKA DIOCESE
Establishment Year: 2003

📍 Location

St Jude Ssanje Health Centre III is located in Kyotera District, Kasasa Sub-county, Ssanje Parish.

It is situated 13.5 kilometers (13½ km) from Kyotera town along the Mutukula Road.

The facility serves the local community and extends services to nearby areas.

📖 Background and Establishment

The health centre was established in 2003 to improve and maintain health care services for the Ssanje community and surrounding areas. Rev. Fr. Edward Nsanziiro spearheaded the initiative in collaboration with the Ssanje Catholic Parish and the Masaka Diocese.

It operates as part of the Masaka Diocesan Medical Services.

🏛️ Accreditation and Supervision

  • Accredited by the Ministry of Health under the Uganda Catholic Medical Bureau.
  • Licensed annually by the Uganda Medical and Dental Practitioners Council.
  • Directly supervised by the Diocesan Health Coordinator (DHC) of Masaka Diocese and the District Health Officer (DHO) of Kyotera District.

👥 Population Served

The health centre serves a population of over 20,000 people from the following sub-counties:

  • Kasasa sub-county
  • Kyebe sub-county
  • Kifamba sub-county
  • Kakuuto sub-county

Generational Farms Development Initiative

Building Today, Sustaining Generations.

About GFDI

Generational Farms Development Initiative (GFDI) is a non-profit, non-political and non-sectarian community-based development organisation seeking to build productive livelihoods, knowledge, healthy communities and resilient natural resources whose benefits can continue across generations.

GFDI is established at district level as a Community-Based Organisation (CBO), initially operating in Kyotera District, Uganda.

GFDI helps with farm hosting, volunteer programs, starting farms on others' land with permission, taking care of these farms for both international and local farmers, hosting volunteers both local and international, farm planning, farm planting, farm management, and internship placement.

👁️ Vision

A prosperous, empowered and resilient society where people, farms, communities and natural resources thrive across generations.

🎯 Mission

To empower communities and individuals to build sustainable livelihoods and resilient futures through agriculture, education, enterprise development, environmental conservation, health, skills development and inclusive community transformation.

💎 Core Values

✝️ Integrity and honesty
📋 Accountability and transparency
👥 Community participation and ownership
🌱 Sustainability and stewardship
⚖️ Equality, inclusion and dignity
🤝 Service before self-interest
💡 Innovation and learning
🌍 Respect for people and nature
📦 Responsible use of resources

🎯 Overall Goal

To contribute to sustainable social, economic and environmental development by investing in people, productive livelihoods, agriculture, communities and natural resources for the benefit of present and future generations.

📋 Objectives

  • Promote sustainable and climate-smart agriculture and resilient food systems.
  • Improve household food and nutrition security.
  • Promote coffee and other agricultural value chains, including quality improvement, traceability, value addition and market access.
  • Support farmer training, demonstration farms, farm recordkeeping and practical agricultural innovation.
  • Promote agroforestry, tree planting, soil and water conservation and environmental restoration.
  • Equip young people with agricultural, vocational, entrepreneurial, digital and leadership skills.
  • Promote women's economic empowerment, enterprise development and participation in leadership.
  • Support community enterprises, savings initiatives, cooperatives and responsible access to markets.
  • Promote education, community learning, research, knowledge exchange and innovation.
  • Promote health, sanitation, hygiene, nutrition and community wellbeing.
  • Support vulnerable households and inclusive participation of persons with disabilities and other marginalised groups.
  • Mobilise lawful resources and partnerships for community development.
  • Work constructively with government, private sector, civil society, academic institutions, farmer organisations and development partners.
  • Promote livelihoods and productive assets that benefit present and future generations.
